RavenMap

A privacy-first, community-owned situational-awareness and public-accountability network.

Observe carefully. Remember responsibly. Make public power accountable without turning private lives into public records.

What RavenMap is

RavenMap is an open-source platform for collecting, correlating, reviewing, and publishing observations that have legitimate public-accountability or situational-awareness value. It is designed for communities that want to operate their own sensors and infrastructure rather than depend on a single commercial surveillance provider or central authority.

The project began with vehicle observations from community-operated cameras, and that remains an important capability. RavenMap is broader than a license plate map, though: a deployment can combine edge cameras, public traffic cameras, public data sources, manually submitted observations, and other authorized sensor inputs while keeping their provenance and trust boundaries distinct.

The goal is not to collect as much as possible. The goal is to gather information that serves a defined purpose, preserve what is justified, discard what is not, and make the rules visible enough that a deployment's claims can be checked.

Why ravens?

RavenMap takes its name from Huginn and Muninn, the ravens of Odin. Their names are commonly rendered as Thought and Memory: they range outward, observe the world, and return with what they have learned.

That is a useful model for a distributed observation network. Sensors and contributors gather observations in many places; RavenMap brings those observations together, adds context, preserves what matters, and returns useful knowledge to the community operating it.

The second half of the idea matters just as much: observation without restraint becomes surveillance. RavenMap is therefore built around judgment, minimization, and explicit trust boundaries, not the assumption that every technically collectible fact should become a permanent record.

Watching the watchers without watching everyone

Government agencies and private vendors increasingly use automated sensors, cameras, databases, and analytics to observe public spaces. RavenMap exists in part to make public power observable in return: public vehicles, public operations, and other legitimate accountability subjects can be documented and examined by the communities they affect.

That mission does not justify building a searchable history of ordinary people's movements. RavenMap therefore treats public-accountability data and private-person data as different classes with different handling rules.

Public-accountability tier

  • Subjects that meet a deployment's public-accountability policy
  • Readable identifiers may be retained when publication criteria are met
  • Evidence and provenance can support public review
  • Retention and publication follow explicit deployment policy
  • Public records are intended to be inspectable rather than secret

Private tier

  • Ordinary private persons and vehicles
  • Readable plate text is not intentionally persisted centrally
  • Retained imagery is minimized and identifying plate data is redacted
  • Correlation is temporary and bounded rather than permanent
  • No public plate-lookup path is provided

Those exact rules are implementation and deployment policy, not a slogan. RavenMap exposes policy and transparency information so they can be examined rather than merely trusted.

Privacy is part of the architecture

Raw video stays at the edge by default. RavenMap's normal model is local processing followed by an authorized observation or sanitized still, not continuous raw video sent to a central archive.

Retained media should contain only what is needed. Vehicle observations normally use tight crops and private-tier imagery is subject to redaction so unrelated people, homes, and identifiers are not collected simply because they happened to be in the frame.

Private correlation is bounded. Where temporary re-identification is operationally useful, private identifiers are designed to be pseudonymous and time-limited rather than a permanent trail that can be reconstructed later.

Publication is conservative. A single OCR result or weak classifier should not be enough to turn an ordinary person's observation into a public record. Publication decisions require the evidence and confidence appropriate to the deployment's policy.

Trust is not universal. Operators, reviewers, nodes, public users, and processing workers are different roles. Access to compute capacity does not automatically grant access to sensitive observations.

What RavenMap is not

RavenMap is not intended to become a warehouse of everyone's raw video, an indiscriminate license-plate database, a permanent searchable history of private citizens, or a centralized commercial surveillance service.

It is also not an enforcement system. RavenMap's purpose is observation, context, review, transparency, and accountability. Data collection is not automatically valuable merely because a sensor or model is capable of it.

Open and verifiable

RavenMap is open source. Its privacy rules, publication decisions, APIs, and implementation can be examined rather than accepted as a black box. A deployment should expose enough about its own operation to let users, contributors, and researchers evaluate whether it behaves as claimed without exposing the private information those safeguards exist to protect.

Project lineage

RavenMap began as a fork of SparrowMap and retains substantial inherited code and ideas from that project. SparrowMap remains part of RavenMap's provenance and must be credited accordingly, but RavenMap is a separate project with its own name, direction, architecture, and operational identity.
