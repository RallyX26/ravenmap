Turn a camera into a node

An old phone in a window, or a laptop with its webcam, or a USB camera on a desktop — any device with a camera and a browser. Nothing to install and no account to make. Detection runs on this device; no video is uploaded, ever. One small crop per vehicle goes to the map, deliberately shrunk below the size a licence plate can be read at, and the server checks the size rather than taking this page's word for it.

What this phone sends

  • A crop of each vehicle, 200 px at most on its longest side.
  • The time, and your camera's approximate position — the map jitters it, and sightings are placed on the road, not on you.
  • Not video. Not full frames. Not a readable plate. Not anything when nothing is moving.

Leaving it running

  • Keep it plugged in. Detection uses the processor continuously and will flatten a laptop or phone battery in a couple of hours.
  • Leave this tab in front. Browsers suspend background tabs, and a suspended tab sees nothing. On a desktop you can leave it in its own window behind everything else — it only has to be visible, not focused.
  • On battery, it stops itself below 20% rather than killing the device.
  • Wedge it against the glass. Reflections at night are the usual reason a window camera sees nothing useful.
