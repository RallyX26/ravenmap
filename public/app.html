Set up this camera

Any device with a camera and a browser — a phone in a window, a laptop, a USB webcam. Nothing to install, no account. Detection runs here; no video is ever uploaded.

SparrowMap does not keep your exact location. It is used only to work out which road your camera watches — the map stores just that road and a point blurred by about 60 m, never your address or your precise spot.

Already have a camera elsewhere? Scan its key with this device's camera app, or open the key file from a USB stick.

What downloads, and why

A 38 MB vehicle detector — the open-source model your phone uses to recognise vehicles on the device. It is the reason your video never leaves this phone: there is no server doing the looking, so there is nothing to upload.

  • Nothing is installed. It is a file this web page loads, like an image. Closing the tab ends it.
  • It comes from this site, not a third party.
  • Once only. Your browser caches it, so every later start is instant.
  • On mobile data it is a big download — Wi-Fi is kinder.

Leaving it running

  • Keep it plugged in.
  • Leave this window visible — browsers freeze hidden tabs, and a frozen tab sees nothing. It does not need to be focused.
  • On battery it stops itself at 20% rather than killing the device.
  • Wedge it against the glass; reflections are the usual reason a window camera sees nothing at night.

This camera's key

An account without an account.

Photograph this with another device to give it control of this camera. No sign-in, nothing to remember.

Whoever holds this key controls the camera. That is what makes it anonymous — there is no account tying it to a person. It also means a photograph of this screen is enough. Treat it like a house key.

Rotating mints a new key and kills every copy of the old one, including any you printed or shared.

The secret sits after a # in the link, and browsers never send that part to a server. Scanning a key transmits nothing to us. We cannot recover it for you, because we do not have it.
